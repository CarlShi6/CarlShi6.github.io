Build Hope — Content Operations System

A five-day client sprint to help a nonprofit turn existing event media into a repeatable content workflow.

Engagement
Four-person team · Five-day client sprint · September 2026
Role
Product Strategy & Operations Consultant
Context
Client engagement through USC Iovine and Young Academy
01 · Challenge

Mission work generated meaningful stories, but the content process was difficult to sustain.

Build Hope’s small team was coordinating social content manually while also delivering programs and supporting its community. The engagement focused on making that work more repeatable without replacing the human judgment and lived experience behind each story.

02 · Reframing the problem

The core need was an operating system—not simply more posts.

The team already had programs, events, updates, and community impact worth sharing. The missing layer was a reliable path for turning those inputs into planned, approved, published, and learnable content.

03 · Operational gaps

Four capabilities were not covered by the existing workflow.

01

Automated publishing

Prepare and schedule content without repeating the same manual steps for every channel.

02

Social analytics

Bring performance signals together so the team can identify useful patterns.

03

Content ideation

Maintain a practical backlog built from programs, events, milestones, and community stories.

04

Cross-platform management

Coordinate multiple social channels from a shared operational view.

04 · Solution evaluation

Four platforms were compared against the team’s operating needs.

Our team researched roughly eight options and highlighted Metricool, monday.com, Buffer, and Agorapulse in the presentation. I personally tested Buffer, Metricool, and monday.com. The recommendation moved from Buffer to Metricool after considering analytics, engagement insights, automation, usability, and cost; this was a qualitative comparison, not a formal scoring matrix.

05 · Operating model

Ideate → Plan → Publish → Learn

01

Ideate

Turn programs, events, milestones, and community stories into a usable content backlog.

02

Plan

Choose priorities, owners, channels, and publishing dates in a shared calendar.

03

Publish

Prepare and schedule channel-ready content through a centralized workflow.

04

Learn

Review performance, identify useful patterns, and feed those insights into the next cycle.

06 · 30-day implementation roadmap

Adoption is sequenced as a manageable first operating cycle.

Days 1–2

Connect platforms, review recent performance, and establish a baseline.

Days 3–5

Centralize assets and tag them by program, audience, and content category.

Days 6–7

Select content, build the calendar, and establish a review rhythm.

Days 8–21

Schedule, publish, and monitor the first operating cycle.

Days 22–30

Review top content, compare formats, and apply insights to the next cycle.

07 · Content playbook / feedback loop

A repeatable playbook connects creation to learning.

The playbook moves through Define, Gather, Create, Organize, Publish, and Learn. Its feedback loop—Create → Publish → Measure → Learn → Improve → Repeat—helps each cycle inform the next one.

08 · Proposed impact

Less administrative work. More consistent storytelling. Greater visibility.

These are proposed outcomes, not measured post-launch results. The final delivery defines the workflow and expected value; it does not document validated changes in engagement, reach, productivity, or revenue.

09 · My role / reflection

Product Strategy & Operations Consultant — Build Hope

In this four-person team, I pushed our discovery beyond views and posting frequency by asking the stakeholder about funding, engagement, priorities, and staff constraints. I designed the five-phase, 30-day roadmap, contributed the content playbook and handoff material, and proposed testing TikTok with existing event media under a standardized workflow. I presented the roadmap and Metricool features. Stakeholders responded positively, expressed interest in trying Metricool, and specifically liked the roadmap as a clear reference. Adoption and performance changes were not measured.

AI PC Build Advisor

An AI-assisted prototype that turns a beginner's needs into part recommendations, comparisons, compatibility guidance, and a price-aware build list.

Role
Technical Product Lead · AI-assisted product prototype
Status
Paused prototype · Not publicly deployed
Research
3–5 user interviews · Competitor research
Core flow
Need → Recommendation → Review → Purchase
Prototype scope
Guided advisor · Comparison · Compatibility · Handoff
01 · Problem

What the product needs to make clear.

Choosing PC parts is still overwhelming for beginners. Specifications are difficult to interpret, similar components are hard to compare, compatibility and price risks are easy to miss, and advice across different sources often conflicts.

I interviewed 3–5 potential users and reviewed PCPartPicker, Newegg, Micro Center, Best Buy, and other PC-building resources before development and during iteration. I defined the MVP, prioritized the core decision flows, and deferred retailer integrations to keep the prototype focused.

02 · System

The logic inside this project.

  1. 01

    Understand

    A chat-based intake captures budget, games, software, aesthetic preferences, brand preferences, and performance needs.

  2. 02

    Generate

    The advisor converts those needs into a complete component set with budget and performance context.

  3. 03

    Guard

    Compatibility rules check CPU socket, motherboard support, GPU and cooling clearance, PSU wattage, and case fit.

  4. 04

    Handoff

    The selected configuration carries its parts and updated total into a purchase reference list. Retailer integrations were deferred.

03 · Key decisions

Make expert logic understandable to beginners.

  1. 01

    Needs before specs

    Start with budget, workload, and style—not hardware terminology.

  2. 02

    Rule-based compatibility

    AI explains; explicit rules check sockets, power, clearance, and fit.

  3. 03

    See the trade-off

    Show price and performance changes before replacing a part.

  4. 04

    Prepare a parts list

    Keep component choices and the updated total visible; retailer integrations remained outside MVP scope.

04 · Execution

From guided intake to compatibility-safe replacement.

05 · Final experience

One continuous workspace from recommendation to purchase.

The prototype keeps recommendations, compatibility warnings, comparison of up to three parts, replacement, and the updated build total in one workspace. I reviewed AI-generated code, diagnosed issues, and tested incompatible configurations and key flows myself. The chatbot explains products and components; real-time price-value judgment remains a limitation.

06 · Outcome

The paused prototype demonstrates a decision path from guided needs to part recommendations, compatibility review, comparison, replacement, and an updated build list. It was not publicly deployed and has no post-launch adoption results.

Dog Behavior Camera

An independent Raspberry Pi computer vision prototype showing real-time dog detection and basic behavioral-state feedback, with state-to-actuator response logic.

Role
Independent extracurricular project
Tools
Python · OpenCV · YOLO · Raspberry Pi
Scope
Adapted existing framework and labeled dataset · Summer 2025
01 · Problem

What the product needs to make clear.

Daily interactions such as feeding, playing, resting, and standing contain behavioral signals that are difficult for an owner to interpret consistently. The project explores how vision and motion detection can make those signals visible and support a more responsive relationship.

02 · Project system

The logic inside this project.

  1. 01

    Observe

    Use camera input to establish the dog’s current position, movement, and activity context.

  2. 02

    Interpret

    Adjust detection parameters, weighting, temporal buffering, and post-processing to show basic behavioral-state feedback on screen.

  3. 03

    Respond

    Design the logic connecting a persistent detected state to a physical actuator response.

  4. 04

    Validate

    Test with a real dog; keep the broader enclosure, communication, and food modules labeled as concepts.

03 · Evidence

The system made visible.

04 · Outcome

Testing with a real dog demonstrated on-screen detection and basic behavioral-state feedback. The model weights were not fine-tuned; the larger companion device and remote features remained concepts.

Luggage Helper

A mobile prototype for planning a trip, estimating packed weight, reviewing potential item restrictions, and keeping a packing checklist.

Focus
Smart luggage management
Journey
Before · During · After travel
Output
Weight · Warnings · Checklist
01 · Problem

What the product needs to make clear.

Baggage allowances and item restrictions change across routes, countries, and airlines. Travelers often discover weight limits too late, estimate luggage capacity by hand, or learn at inspection that an item cannot be carried.

02 · Project system

The logic inside this project.

  1. 01

    Create

    Start a trip with departure, destination, and the applicable luggage weight limit.

  2. 02

    Capture

    Add an item through a photo, image upload, or text description.

  3. 03

    Analyze

    Send the item context to the model and return an estimated weight and travel-safety classification.

  4. 04

    Guide

    Update the trip total, surface overweight or restricted-item warnings, and maintain a packing checklist.

03 · Evidence

The system made visible.

04 · Outcome

The mobile prototype connects trip setup, item input, estimated weight, potential restriction warnings, and a checklist. It is not a released travel service or an authoritative source of airline and customs rules.

Music Pulse

An installation concept for a Brooklyn park, bringing music, movement, and responsive visuals into everyday public space.

Context
Brooklyn · Urban park
Interaction
Music + movement → visuals
Stage
Installation concept · Rhino / KeyShot
01 · Problem

What the product needs to make clear.

A nearby park is not always a welcoming place. Music Pulse explores how a low-barrier, shared creative activity could invite people to pause, participate, and feel more connected to their neighborhood.

02 · Project system

The logic inside this project.

  1. 01

    Choose

    Select music and visualization features, or let the system play a random track.

  2. 02

    Move

    The proposed system captures movement from participants and passersby.

  3. 03

    Visualize

    The display translates music and movement into a responsive particle image.

  4. 04

    Gather

    Sound and a visible shared screen make the interaction part of the park experience.

03 · Evidence

The system made visible.

04 · Outcome

A modeled installation concept connecting music, bodily participation, and public-space design. The portfolio documents the design and intended experience, not a deployed installation or measured community impact.
